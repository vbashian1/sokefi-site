Do you ever have one of those moments when something hits you and you just have to take action? I do, and I have to admit that my family and friends think I'm crazy at times, but spontaneity is one of the great joys of life, right? It's what made me, as a young university student who had just seen the Stones at Wembley, buy the T-shirt and instantly know what I was going to do that summer. I would go where the Stones went. My goal? To see as many shows as I could. Impulsive? Definitely! But what a summer it was!

<PullQuote variant="display">Sometimes, we need to remember not to overthink.</PullQuote>

I was reminded of that moment on a recent trip to one of America's great national parks. We'd stopped at the entrance, like so many others do, to get a photo by the park sign when two ladies asked me to take their photo. As you'd expect, this request to me was an invitation to strike up a bigger conversation. As it turned out, these two ladies, from the state of Ohio, were several states away from home on a cross-country adventure and having the time of their lives. They'd left their husbands behind and set out — they said — because it was just too hot to stay home. These were my kind of people — spontaneous and looking for any excuse to go! They told me that their husbands and friends had told them they were crazy to drive across the United States in their 1989 Econoline van, but they were determined. They said if not now, when? They were in their mid-60s and were tired of waiting for the perfect time to travel.

<PullQuote variant="side" side="left">Instead of finding reasons to say no, we look for ways to say yes.</PullQuote>

Their sense of adventure stayed with me as we drove through the park — two midlife women who'd said yes, when it would have probably been easier to say no. To me spontaneity is one of life's great joys — that sense of not knowing what might be ahead but making the decision to find out without thinking too much about it. It's something that seems to come more naturally when we're young, but then life comes along and we become more cautious — there are career paths to consider and mortgages to pay. When children come along, they take centre stage and life naturally becomes more measured — and less spontaneous. As we move through our careers and retirement looms — or maybe has already arrived — being deliberate is necessary, but if we aren't careful — no pun intended — that same caution can become so ingrained that it can override everything we do. Sometimes, we need to remember not to overthink — after all, we're at that age where if we don't do things now, will we ever?

Based on the rusted old Econoline van and their stories of camping along the way across the country, the ladies from Ohio were not wealthy in the traditional sense. What they were, though, was rich in experience — they challenged the norms that would have suggested that they wait until they could get a better van or until their husbands were ready to travel and instead just packed up and went. In retrospect, it's not much different from what I did as a young university student. I certainly didn't have much money, but what I did have was a Eurail pass and a sense of adventure. That summer I travelled across Europe, ready to explore new cities when I arrived. I met people from all over the world — many, like me, were in town to see the Rolling Stones — others were just there to see the sights. It was a summer to remember and one that set me on a path of spontaneity and adventure that continues today.

<PullQuote>Two midlife women who'd said yes, when it would have been easier to say no.</PullQuote>

No wonder I felt that instant connection with the ladies from Ohio. Their sense of spontaneity and seizing the moment is what Sokéfi is all about. It's rediscovering that sense of living in the moment — the notion that instead of finding reasons to say no, we look for ways to say yes. It doesn't have to mean jumping into a dilapidated old van and driving thousands of miles stopping at campgrounds en route — it just means recapturing the spirit of our youth where we said yes, because why would we say no? A time when a Eurail pass and a concert T-shirt with dates and locations for the next shows could provide a map for summer travel. It's that idea of living for now. What are you waiting for?
