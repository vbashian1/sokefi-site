That old cabin has been a getaway for years — my wife and I first going as newlyweds and over the years returning again and again as our family grew from just the two of us to six. Somehow the little cabin was able to expand just enough each time.

We don't go as much as we used to — as the kids have got older, schedules are busier and more challenging to coordinate — and the cabin is in need of some updates. Those old beds don't seem quite as comfortable for a middle-aged back as they did when we were newlyweds.

We've thought about changing it — adding new windows to let more light in, clearing out a closet filled with who knows what to create enough space to squeeze in a second bathroom, making much-needed updates to the plumbing and electrical systems — but we never have. Other things take priority — there were school fees to pay, family trips to take, repairs on our main home to make — and so our little cabin remains much the same as it always has.

On a recent trip there — unusual because we were all able to go at the same time, something we hadn't achieved for a couple of years — we quickly fell back into our cabin ways. The careful choreography of getting six adults ready in the morning when there's only a single bathroom, running as smoothly as ever. Setting out on the familiar, yet ever-changing hikes. Swimming in the icy cold water of the nearby lake. Perfection, as only a place like this can be — a refuge from the busyness of the world, a place where you can just be. As we sat around the fire on the last night of our trip, I knew, at that moment, that despite all of its flaws, that cabin in the woods brings us together in a way that nothing else can. It was, as they say, a perfect Sokéfi moment — a feeling of utter joy and contentment.
