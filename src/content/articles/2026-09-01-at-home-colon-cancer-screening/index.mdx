Getting a colonoscopy is probably not at the top of the list of fun activities for most people. Yet colonoscopies are a key tool in the fight against colon cancer and it's generally recommended that U.S. adults over the age of 45 get an initial baseline colonoscopy and then schedule additional tests every 10 years. Despite the fact that most U.S. insurers cover the cost of the exam, not everyone follows the recommended advice.

The incidence of colon cancer across the United States, and especially among younger people, has risen sharply in recent years. Treating colon cancer is not easy and, as with many types of cancer, survival rates tend to be higher when it's caught earlier.

Now, a new way to screen for colon cancer could play a major role in how we fight the disease. Cologuard — now in an updated version called Cologuard Plus — is an FDA-approved test to screen for cancer in those over the age of 45 at average risk of the disease. Because it's a non-invasive test that you complete in the comfort of your own home, it's anticipated that there will be greater take up among people, and especially among those who might avoid the standard colonoscopy. This could then mean that more people are screened earlier, when, if it's detected, the cancer is easier to treat.

<PullQuote variant="display">Survival rates tend to be higher when it's caught earlier.</PullQuote>

Cologuard has a 95 percent detection rate. If a positive result is found, the patient should go for further testing. If the result is negative, it's recommended that the test be completed again in 3 years. Currently, Cologuard is covered by most major insurers and Medicare in the United States.

We encourage you to talk to your health care providers about Cologuard. We're not doctors, but we want to help raise awareness of new developments in the fight against cancer, and the steps that can help everyone live well.
