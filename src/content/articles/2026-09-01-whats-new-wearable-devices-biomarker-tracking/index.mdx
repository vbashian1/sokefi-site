Wearable devices that track everything from our sleep patterns to our blood oxygen saturation have become common in recent years. One of the newest to hit the market is the Hilo Core, a device designed to continuously monitor blood pressure. The device, or wearable as some may refer to it, recently gained FDA clearance.

The Hilo Core joins a string of other wearable devices, more commonly referred to as simply wearables, that have become increasingly popular among those interested in more closely monitoring their health and focusing on the biomarkers that can provide detailed stats on key health metrics. Some, like the Hilo Core, a cuffless continuous blood pressure monitor, are designed for specific purposes, while others, such as the Oura Ring, provide statistics across a broader spectrum.

It's easy to understand why products like the Hilo Core and the Oura Ring are gaining traction, especially among midlifers. Longevity in general and taking steps now to improve our health as we age has become a central focus to many in a population where record numbers of people are now past the mid-century mark.

Wearable devices can provide information almost instantly, and in doing so, enable users to more quickly and easily respond to issues as they emerge. In the case of the Hilo Core, this has the potential to be lifesaving. High blood pressure is often referred to as the silent killer. It tends to develop gradually, often starting as elevated blood pressure — the stage before full hypertension. Even people who are active and at a healthy weight can be living with hypertension because it can affect virtually anyone, and its symptoms often go unrecognised. In many cases hypertension can be resolved or improved by making relatively simple changes to diet and exercise. The idea behind the Hilo Core is that it could alert users to their risk of high blood pressure and the need to take the necessary steps to reduce their risk of heart disease.
